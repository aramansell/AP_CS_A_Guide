10.5c — RPG Pathfinding & Course Retrospective

Season 2: beyond the exam. This topic is no longer tested (the 2025 CED removed inheritance from the exam). We build it after May 12 because it makes the RPG engine dramatically better, and because you are ready for it. Pure craft, zero test anxiety.
Expand Application ~25 min

Part 1: Monster Chase AI

Your dungeon has a monster that chases the player. The monster needs to find the shortest path. Use recursive backtracking to find a path, then have the monster take the first step along it.

M = Monster  P = Player  # = Wall

. M . # .
. # . # P
. . . # .
# . # . .
. . . . .

Monster finds path: [(1,1)→(2,1)→(2,2)→(1,2)→(1,3)→(1,4)]
Monster moves one step: to (2,1)

. . . # .
. # M # P
. . . # .
# . # . .
. . . . .

Requirements:

  • Monster moves one cell per turn toward the player
  • If no path exists (player is walled off), monster wanders randomly
  • If monster reaches the player → combat!

Socratic Checkpoint

Finding the full path every turn is wasteful — the monster only takes one step. Could you use a simpler algorithm? What if the monster just moves toward the player's row/column, one step at a time? When would that fail? Design a maze where "move toward" gets stuck but backtracking succeeds.

Part 2: N-Queens Problem

Place N queens on an N×N chessboard so no two queens attack each other. Queens attack along rows, columns, and diagonals.

4×4 solutions:
. Q . .    . . Q .
. . . Q    Q . . .
Q . . .    . . . Q
. . Q .    . Q . .

Backtracking approach: place one queen per column. For column C, try every row. If the placement is safe (no other queen in the same row or diagonal), recurse to column C+1. If no row works, backtrack to column C-1.

Tinker: RPG Equipment Constraints

Your player has 4 equipment slots (head, chest, hands, feet) and 8 items. Some items conflict: you can't wear a helmet AND a crown (both head slot). You can't wear chainmail AND a robe (both chest). Use backtracking to find all valid equipment combinations that maximize total armor rating. This is the exact same problem as N-Queens, just with different "attack" rules.

Part 3: Course Retrospective

Build a "player stats" screen that shows everything you've wired in over 10 units:

  • Character: Name, HP, XP, level — built with classes (Unit 5)
  • Inventory: ArrayList of Items with names, weights, values (Unit 7)
  • Combat Log: Last 10 fights stored in a fixed-size array (Unit 6)
  • Dungeon Map: 2D array with fog-of-war overlay (Unit 8)
  • Item Drops: Weighted loot table (Unit 7)
  • Pathfinding: Monster AI that chases (Unit 10)

Write a final reflection (3-5 paragraphs) comparing how you wrote code in Unit 1 (if-else ranges for loot drops, raw formulas for random numbers) to how you write code now (data-driven loot tables, class-based architecture, recursive algorithms). What's the biggest shift in how you think about programming?

Course Completion Checkpoint:

  • Monster uses recursive pathfinding to chase the player
  • N-Queens solver using column-by-column backtracking
  • Full RPG integration: character, inventory, combat, dungeon, loot
  • Written reflection on your growth from Unit 1 to Unit 10
